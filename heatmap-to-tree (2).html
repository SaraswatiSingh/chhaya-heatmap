Plant here first.

Hot neighbourhoods with little shade and many residents gain the most from new trees. This tool ranks pixels of a city by how much cooling a tree site would deliver to how many people, under a fixed planting budget.

Prototype on synthetic data. The city below is simulated around Ghaziabad (28.67 N, 77.45 E). The Python pipeline in the project repository swaps in Landsat temperature, Sentinel/Landsat vegetation indices and WorldPop population from Google Earth Engine.

    Zones are a 3×3 split of the map. Real ward boundaries would replace them.

    Cooling is land surface temperature in the planted pixel, which is larger than the change in air temperature people feel. CO₂ assumes 25 trees per site and 21 kg per tree per year. Treat it as a rough estimate, not a measurement.

    How it works

    A regression model learns how temperature depends on vegetation, built-up area and nearness to water. For each pixel it then asks what would change if vegetation reached a healthy tree-cover level, and the drop is the cooling. Sites are chosen greedily by cooling times people, with a minimum gap so they spread across the city.

    Three additions go beyond a plain heat map. Schools and hospitals can be given extra priority. The cooling of each tree site spills over to nearby pixels (assumed 25% at one pixel and 10% at two), which drives the After planting layer. A zone report then shows which parts of the city stay hottest after the plan.

    Clean-air mode reflects field studies: green barriers can lower particle levels beside roads, but only when designed well. Near busy roads the plan recommends a hedge-and-tree mix (hedges under 2 m) instead of tall dense trees alone. This reduces exposure for people next to roads. It does not lower the whole city’s AQI, which is driven mainly by traffic, dust and burning.

    Inspired by Tree Equity Score and Google’s Tree Canopy Lab. This prototype adds budgeted, spaced site selection and a before-and-after view, aimed at Indian cities where I could not find an equivalent open tool.

    Limits: real planting also depends on land ownership, soil, water supply and species choice, so use the ranking as a shortlist to check on the ground.
